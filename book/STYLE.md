# How the book is written

1. **Every program is real.** A program in the book is a file in
   `testing/tutti-book/examples/`, built by CI, and included by anchor
   (`{{#include ../../testing/tutti-book/examples/a_sound.rs:session}}` with
   `// ANCHOR: session` and `// ANCHOR_END: session` in the file). A snippet
   that is not a whole program is `rust, ignore` and is copied from a real
   file. Lines a reader does not need are hidden with `# `.
2. **Every sound is on the page.** An example that makes sound renders it
   with tutti's own bounce to `book/src/sounds/<name>.flac`, and the page
   plays it with `<audio controls src="sounds/<name>.flac"></audio>` right
   after the code. "You should hear ..." says what to listen for. The sounds
   are generated by `cargo xtask book` and never committed.
3. **A page has one shape.** One sentence of what we build and why; the code;
   "What is going on here", a numbered list with one point per new idea, each
   naming the type as a link to its rustdoc and to its building-block chapter;
   and something to try (change one number, hear the difference).
4. **Small before large.** Inside a building-block chapter, the smallest
   piece comes first (a node, a port, an edge), then what composes them, then
   the session call that does it for you. A term is defined before it is used.
5. **Voice.** "You" for the reader, "we" for the authors, the present tense,
   plain sentences, no marketing. The words mean what they mean in the code:
   a *frame* is one value per channel, a *sample* is one value.
6. **Asides fold away.** Rust background goes in
   `<details><summary>🦀 ...</summary>`, audio background in
   `<details><summary>🎧 ...</summary>`. A rule that bites is a blockquote
   that opens with a bold sentence: "> **Never allocate on the audio
   thread.**"
7. **The book teaches; rustdoc is the reference.** A chapter links the items
   it uses and does not restate their docs.
